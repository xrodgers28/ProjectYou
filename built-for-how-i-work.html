Built For How I Work

v1.0
Project YOU is not a general productivity system that happens to be yours. Almost every design decision in it was made around how your attention actually behaves. Most of them do not look like ADHD features. They look like ordinary product choices. This page is the reasoning underneath, so a future you, or anyone you show it to, can see why the system is shaped this way rather than some other way.

Eleven decisions, and why

One

The system remembers, so you do not have to

The biggest one. Every idea gets captured the moment it lands: you talk into your watch, it arrives in your to-do system, it gets sorted into the right place. Nothing waits on you holding it in your head.

"I can look at a map but two minutes later forget where I am going."

Where it shows up: the capture pipeline, the Staging Area, the inbox, and the rule that no input is ever dropped.
Two

Click, do not type

Typing is friction and friction kills momentum. So decisions arrive as buttons, tick-boxes and yes/no taps. When a question needs answering, it comes as a set of choices with the recommendation already selected, not as a paragraph asking you to compose a reply.

Where it shows up: every approval sheet, the Session Tracker's Got It buttons, and the tap-a-number scoring on the Nightly Scorecard.
Three

Nothing big is ever handed over whole

Large vague tasks stall before they start. So work always arrives broken into a concrete next action, with the single next step named rather than implied.

"Have a hard time deciding where to start."

Where it shows up: the numbered "your move" at the end of every update, and the What You Do Next column on the tracker.
Four

Time gets an external clock

You underestimate how long things take, and knowing that does not fix it. So estimates get recorded next to actuals, everything is timestamped, and the day has bookends. The system keeps time so you can stay in the work rather than watching the clock.

"I underestimate and am frustrated with how long everything takes."

Where it shows up: minute estimates in brackets on every task, the activity log, and the status check and wrap rituals.
Five

Everything gets a permanent address

Out of sight, out of mind applies to entire projects, not just small things. So nothing important lives only inside a conversation that scrolls away. Every page has a URL and Mission Control links to all of it.

Where it shows up: this page, and the standing rule that anything built gets added to the Mission Control board.
Six

Novelty is fed, not fought

Boredom is not a discipline problem to be overcome, it is a fact to design around. So content rotates: cards deal something new each day, the nightly questions change, and learning is shaped as a game rather than a reading list. A thing that stays identical every day will be abandoned, however good it is.

"I get bored easily. It's more fun to start a new project."

Where it shows up: the daily card rotation, the rotating nightly questions, and Corpus Recall.
Seven

Structure instead of willpower

The Nightly Scorecard is the clearest example. Same questions, same order, ninety seconds, and a forgiven night when you miss one. It does not ask you to be disciplined. It asks you to tap six numbers.

Where it shows up: the Nightly Scorecard, the habit board, and the grace night built into every streak.
Eight

Effort is scored, never outcome

Every nightly question begins "did I do my best to", which puts the answer entirely inside your control. A bad day then produces a low number instead of a verdict about who you are. That distinction is the difference between a tracker you keep and one you quietly abandon after a rough week.

"I often feel like I'm underachieving in my life, not living up to my potential."

Where it shows up: the wording of all fifteen nightly questions, taken from Marshall Goldsmith's own practice.
Nine

Someone tells you when you are drifting

When a fourth chat opens on the same job, or a new word appears for something already named, or a request lands that an existing tool already handles, you get told in one line and the work carries on. An external brake, because the internal one does not fire in time.

Where it shows up: the call-out rule, added August 2026 at your request. It absorbed two earlier rules that were saying the same thing differently.
Ten

Your strengths are the engine, not a footnote

The working rules open by naming what you are good at: fast divergent idea generation, hyperfocus once something grabs you, and spotting patterns across unrelated things. The system's job is to hold the detail so you can stay at the vision level, and to get out of the way entirely when a good run is happening.

Where it shows up: the superpowers table at the top of the working rules, before the challenges table.
Eleven

The book itself is in the corpus
