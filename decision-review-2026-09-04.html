Physical Health: the gap is not really a missing card

You asked me to draft one cue card for the one part of the Compass that has none. Reading your system first changed what I wanted to propose.

You already wrote twenty-three Physical Health habits. Nineteen are switched off.

Physical Health is not short of ideas. It has more habits written down than any other part of the Compass. What it is short of is habits that are actually running. Four are live: three water bottles, take medicine, walk, and lights out by eleven. The other nineteen are sitting there switched off — squats while the kettle boils, one standing block at the desk, a cold finish to the shower, daylight in the eyes within an hour of waking, posture reset, pushups.

The proposal: a card called Body Signals

One card, three tiles a day, in exactly the shape of your other cue cards so it drops into the deck without inventing anything new.

Tiles one and two each take one number your body filed yesterday and put it next to your own sixty-day range, with one plain sentence explaining what the number is and what moves it. Not a chart. A sentence you could repeat to someone.

Heart rate variability Last night 45 Your normal range 41 to 54 The gap between one heartbeat and the next. Wider gaps usually mean the body is rested and ready to work. It drops after a hard day, a late night, or a drink.

Tile three is the one that earns the card its place. It takes one of your nineteen switched-off habits, offers it back in your own words, and gives you a button to turn it on. One habit a day, never the same one twice in a fortnight.

You wrote this one down and switched it off Squats while the kettle boils Daily · two minutes [ Turn it back on ] [ Not this one ]

It reads from what already flows in and from habits you wrote yourself, so there is no new data to gather and nothing new for you to feed. The build is one evening: the carousel is the same one four other cards use, the range maths is small, and the only real work is writing the plain-English sentence for each of the seven measurements, which you would want to read and correct anyway.
