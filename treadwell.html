My impact stories

Nicaragua · Matagalpa highlands

The night the lights came on

My son Max and I rode mules for hours into the Matagalpa highlands to reach San José El Paraíso, a farming community with no road in and no power lines. For a week we woke before dawn and worked beside five local instructors to install rooftop solar on fifteen homes. Max wired the battery, light and outlets for Doña Sara, a widow raising two boys who had never had electricity. When we flipped the switch and her first bulb glowed, the room fell silent, then erupted. We finished all fifteen houses, and the village threw a piñata party to send us off.

💎 Off-grid solar · GRID Alternatives
Nicaragua · Masatepe

A field full of new gloves

I first came to Nicaragua to install solar panels, but it was the kids who changed my plans. In the evenings they pulled me into their games, and when I handed them my glove I realized that in a country where baseball is the national sport, they had never had the gear to play. So I promised to come back. It took months of cold-calls and a lot of rejection before a nonprofit called Helping Kids Round First said yes, and connected me with Noel, a coach in Masatepe who told me exactly what his players needed. When we finally spread the bats, gloves and helmets across the field, the whole town turned out, and the air filled with the crack of bats and the smack of balls into brand-new gloves.

⚾ Sports & youth · Pack for a Purpose
🧶
Peru · Ollantaytambo

A morning at the backstrap loom

In the Sacred Valley town of Ollantaytambo, my daughter Sofia and I spent a morning learning to weave with the Quechua women of the Awamaki co-op. We were hopeless at first, and they laughed and kept guiding our hands. By the end we understood how many hours go into a single strip of cloth, and the fee we paid went straight into the co-op’s own training fund, helping these women run a business on their own terms.

🧶 Textiles & women’s co-op
🐢
Kenya · Watamu

A turtle back to the sea

On the coast at Watamu, my wife Priya and I joined the team at Local Ocean Conservation for a morning of sea-turtle rescue work. We watched a green turtle that had been caught in fishing nets get weighed, checked and cleared for release. When it finally reached the surf and pushed out into the Indian Ocean, the whole beach went quiet, and then cheered it on.

🐘 Wildlife & marine rescue

How activities make the cut

It takes a lot for a TreadWell activity to make the cut

Thousands of “give-back” experiences exist, and most don't survive a closer look. But on TreadWell, before any activity is added to the catalogue, we check where your fee truly goes, who leads the work, and whether the benefit stays in the community.

Thousands of activities researched and sourced from around the globe
From award shortlists and standards bodies to community networks and on-the-ground leads.
Activities are measured for worthiness against
Industry Standards
Judged by the same benchmarks the tourism industry uses. The globally recognised GSTC criteria and Responsible Tourism Award criteria are applied to every listing.
Must pass three accountability gates
Is it led by local people? Does the money reach an accountable local group? Does it stay in the community? All three, or it's out.
TreadWell Approved
Posted activities are vetted, listed, and labelled honestly, with the story of exactly what your visit changes.

If we can't trace the good, we don't list it.

How we vet: standards & credibility
The bar we hold every listing to is built on other people's rigor, not our own say-so.
GSTC↗

Keeper of the globally recognized baseline standards for sustainable tourism: management, socio-economic, cultural and environmental. Positive Footprint builds its worthiness bar on the GSTC criteria rather than inventing a private one, which buys legitimacy and gives operators a target they already recognize.

Visit site →
Responsible Tourism Awards↗

The long-running, expert-judged awards that surface operators genuinely benefiting host communities. Their global winners act as a pre-vetted pipeline of listing leads, already scrutinized by a panel before we assess them against our own tenets.

Visit site →
B Corp↗

Certifies companies meeting verified standards of social and environmental performance, accountability and transparency. We treat B Corp status as a useful supporting signal when vetting an operator's model, never a substitute for checking where the money actually goes.
