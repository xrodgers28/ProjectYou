And the way those three arrived is the newest trap on this page. The daily audit behind this map counts what is in the database by walking its catalog of tables and views. A trigger is not a table and a helper function is not a view, so neither one is ever counted. An entire working subsystem can ship, change what the calendar shows and what the board holds, and the schema count on this page will read exactly the same as it did the day before. There are 38 triggers in public today: three came with the Food Log on the night of Sep 1, 2026, three more with the Asks queue the night after, six more overnight on Sep 3 including photo_shot_to_qs, which is what turns a filed photograph into a tracked habit, two more overnight on Sep 4 that between them let the day planner remember a block you deleted, and two more overnight on Sep 5 that finally tidy the calendar, hiding an event the moment it is cancelled and unhiding its twin when the duplicate is removed. Thirty-six things that have actually bitten

  • A feed that has gone quiet still calls. A feed that has stopped says nothing at all, and the difference is the whole diagnosis. Apple Health went quiet six times in five weeks and came back six times untouched, and every one of those spells still carried posts through the gap with rows_received of zero, which is how this page knew to wait rather than worry. On Sep 17, 2026 there were no posts at all, empty or otherwise, for a whole calendar day. Same flat destination table, completely different fault. Before calling a silent feed quiet, check whether the carrier is still knocking, and if the receipt log is as still as the data table, it is not a slow day.
  • Fixing the count is not the same as fixing the question, and the key you choose decides which one you got. tnt_things saved every entry twelve times because the write was a bare insert; it was repaired inside a day with an upsert, and the table went from 84 rows to 38. But the upsert keys on the entry text rather than on the date, category and slot, so identical words now collapse and a corrected entry still lands as a brand new row beside the wrong one. Breakfast with todd, Breakfast with tidd and Breakfast with Todd all still sit there. A duplicate-collapsing key must name the slot being filled, never the value being put in it, or the table can tell you what was typed and never what was meant.
  • A status view whose healthy band is wider than any outage the feed produces will never once be wrong in a useful direction. health_metric_status printed Healthy. beside eleven metrics through a twenty-four hour outage, a twenty-seven hour outage and now a thirty-one hour stop with a whole day missing, because days_quiet never climbs past 2. Seven outages, seven misses. Calibrate a monitor against the smallest failure you care about, not the largest one you can imagine, and until then read max(created_at) on the table and never the status word.
  • A tile can be skipped while the page around it is being used, and a flat table cannot tell you which happened. On Sep 15, 2026 the day planner took seven blocks, habits were ticked to 9:31pm Eastern and Three New Things took two rows at 10:01pm, while water_glasses, coffee_cups and mf_sessions took nothing at all. From any one of those three tables alone that is indistinguishable from a broken writer. Check what moved beside a still table on the same evening before calling it broken.
  • A clean publish receipt proves the message was accepted, not that the page is live. Between about 5:43pm Eastern on Sep 13, 2026 and 6:00am on Sep 15, three pages were written to pages, fired the hook, and logged ok = true with a repository_dispatch queued note. All three receipts were honest and none of the three pages reached the site, because the half that decompresses them into the repository was not running. A matching row in pages is not proof either, and neither is a clean hook log: both live on this side of the fence and the failure was on the other. The only test that settles it is fetching the file back from the repository and comparing it byte for byte. And nothing anywhere noticed for a day and a half, which is the part still worth fixing.
  • A backfill and a habit are the same rows, and the day column cannot tell you which one you are looking at. On Sep 13, 2026 the day planner, the water tile, the coffee tile and three brand new tables were all filled in for days that had already gone by. The day values read Aug 5 through Sep 13 and describe five weeks. The created_at values all read one evening between 5:25pm and 10:48pm Eastern. This page already had a rule for Apple Health that says judge a feed by when rows arrived rather than by the dates on them, because it backfills oldest-first. The same rule applies to everything logged by hand, and it cuts the other way: there the dates flatter and the arrival times are the honest half. Check both columns on every hand-driven table, every time.
  • Dropping and recreating a view does not fix the fault inside it, and a name-only catalog diff cannot even see that it happened. v_food_day was dropped and rebuilt overnight on Sep 13, 2026. Same name, same columns, new identity in the catalog, and the same fault it has carried for nine days: it still counts water out of water_log, which holds zero rows, rather than water_glasses, which holds twenty-four. The view count did not move and the view names did not change, so only an identity diff showed anything at all. A view being recreated is not the same as a view being corrected.
  • A lane that repairs itself with no receipt teaches you nothing, and you cannot even tell it repaired. sports_ticker stopped refreshing on Sep 8, 2026 and started again at 8:16am Eastern on Sep 11, an hour after this page named it. Nothing in this database logs that job, so there is no way to ask whether it ran and failed for three days, never ran, or was fixed by somebody: the only evidence either way is a timestamp on the row it writes. The same hole sits under Swarm, whose four-times-a-day sync has no log at all, so a week without a coffee stop and a stopped task read identically. Compare that with Strava, which got strava_sync_log on Aug 29, 2026 and can prove a quiet day was quiet. A feed without a receipt cannot report its own outage, and it cannot report its own recovery either. Two lanes on this map are still in that state.
  • An error total is a lifetime counter, so one bad minute reads as a standing fault forever. calendar_sync_log went from 37 logged errors to 100 between Sep 12 and Sep 13, 2026, which is the shape of a lane falling over. All 63 of the new ones came from one pull-detail run at 5:02pm Eastern on Sep 12 that failed every event it touched, and the run three minutes later upserted 58 with no failures. Nothing was lost and nothing needed doing. The column has no expiry and no per-run view beside it, so from tomorrow onward that total will keep saying 100 and keep implying something is broken. Read the last few runs, never the sum, and treat any cumulative error count on this map the same way.
  • Tables that only move when a person taps something all stop together, and the automatic half will cover for them. water_glasses, coffee_cups, meals and photo_shots all end on Sep 9, 2026, day_blocks ends on Sep 10, day_tally stops at Sep 9, and habit_history records zero completions on Sep 11, Sep 12 and Sep 13. Everything with a clock behind it ran straight through the whole window, The Wall dealt its slots, the habit reset wrote its rows, the calendar synced and Apple Health delivered, which is the only reason this reads as four days of not logging rather than an outage. A hand-driven table has no receipt by definition, so there are only two checks: the automatic feeds beside it, and whether anything at all reached the database from the browser. Here 3 calendar events were pasted in from the phone on Sep 12, which needs the same sign-in a habit tick needs, so the write path is provably alive. That still cannot prove a tap was never dropped, and nothing on this map could.
  • A figure that knows its own age is still silent if nothing prints it. sports_ticker was built the right way round: every value carries its own as-of date, kept separate from the row's updated_at, so a standings table from yesterday sitting on a row refreshed this morning says so in the data. Building the honest column is half the job; showing it is the other half.
  • The same fact in two tables is worse than the fact in none. Water is written by the Food Log and Log a Meal into water_log, and by Habit Modules into water_glasses. Both shipped on the night of Sep 3, 2026, nothing joins them, and neither page reads the other's table. Four glasses were logged that evening through Habit Modules, so water_glasses holds four rows, water_log holds none, and the Food Log reports no water for a day the water was actually logged. v_food_day rolls the day up out of water_log alone and so repeats the zero, and no page reads that view, so nothing on the site ever shows the two halves side by side. Coffee is one step behind the same fault: 595 rows of history sit in qs_log and a new empty coffee_cups sits beside them. An empty table is easy to spot; a table that is right about the wrong half is not. Before adding a store for something, search the schema for what already stores it.
  • A shared script that nothing loads has no symptom at all, and it was fixed the next day. needs-you.js was written on Sep 2, 2026 to show the open questions in asks on whatever page you happen to be on. It was finished, in the pages table and in the repo, and on Sep 3 no HTML file on the site referenced it, nor did navpatch.js, nor did the publish Action that injects the navigation everywhere. It is also built to fail silent, so there was nothing to see and nothing to log. It is loaded now: navpatch.js injects it, so it reaches every page the navigation reaches, and all four questions in the queue have since been answered. The lesson outlives the fix, because the only thing that ever found it was a text search of the repo for the file name. The three ways work can be finished and invisible run in order of quietness: a page missing from the navigation can still be reached by URL, a page with no version badge is at least in the navigation, and a shared script nothing loads produces no evidence of any kind. Publishing a script is two steps, and the second one is loading it.
  • A view that returns nothing is usually telling the truth. v_social_fitness shipped on Aug 30, 2026 and returns zero rows. Nothing is wrong with the view: it reads qs_log for a tracker named Social Fitness, and no row in qs_log carries that tracker, because nothing has been logged against the deck yet. The instinct on an empty result is to suspect the join. Check whether the value exists in the source table first, because most of the time the answer is that the thing being measured has not happened.
  • A blocked feed and a quiet feed look identical from the destination table. On Aug 31, 2026 three of the four Reddit sources behind The Wall answered http 403, at 3:24, 3:28 and 3:32am Eastern. The jobs did not error, nothing on the site changed, and wall_items simply did not grow, which is exactly what it does on a morning with nothing new. The only record anywhere is wall_sources.last_pull_note, a text column no page reads. If a feed can be refused, something has to store the refusal, or the outage is unobservable.
  • A page can be live and completely unreachable. wall.html, wall-saved.html and social-fitness.html all shipped on Aug 30, 2026, all sit in the pages table with a payload, and all render. None of the three is listed in nav-config.js, so none appears in the navigation anywhere on the site, and none carries a version badge, so the version sweep cannot see them either. three-new-things.html and now photo-booth.html are in the same state, which makes five, and this has stayed true for six days running. Publishing a page is three steps, not one, and the row count going from 110 to 115 proves only the first.
  • Grants are not policies. service_role once had no write access to any table, so the capture function returned HTTP 200 while storing nothing. Always write one real row as the real role.
  • Supabase will not render HTML. Anything on *.supabase.co is served as text/plain on purpose. That is why GitHub Pages, not the site function, is the live host. A custom domain is the only fix.
  • The local deploy folder goes stale. GitHub is canonical. Editing a local copy and publishing it has silently wiped live sections before.
  • The wheel does not read qs_log. Strava wrote to qs_log for days and nothing appeared, because nothing bridged it to todos. The wheel reads todos plus time_log, and nothing else.
  • Scheduled sessions do reach Supabase, and this page said otherwise for two days. A scheduled cloud session audited and republished this page on Aug 18, 2026 through the ordinary Supabase connector, and the nightly map check writes its findings into session_todos at 3:24am. What a scheduled run cannot reach is the Mac, so it has no Cowork project memory and no local tools. The Midnight Run is still silent, but this was never the reason.
  • A shared table can be empty and still look like it works. deck_progress is how every cue-card page remembers what it already showed you, and for two days it held zero rows. A page with no memory treats every tile as unseen, so the cards render perfectly while the rotation does nothing at all. Nothing errors and nothing looks wrong. The only way to see it was to count the rows. It cleared on Aug 22, 2026 and now holds 115 rows across five decks, with 4 rows in deck_state, which means four decks have been read all the way to the end. The whole thing works now; it is kept here because the failure mode was invisible, not because it is still live.
  • A row count going up is not proof the page is being used. safaris went from 1 row to 18 in two days, which reads like adoption. In fact 17 of those carry source = manual and were loaded straight through the database; only one came from anyone typing into the page. Check source before you read growth as usage.
  • A metric can exist and still be empty. health_metrics is watched for 16 metrics and only 13 have ever arrived: weight, body fat and lean body mass have never appeared at all, and two more are still flagged thin, mindful minutes at 3 readings and resting heart rate at 31. Environmental Health was built on time_in_daylight, which has crept up to 30 and now reads as healthy, which shows how low that threshold sits. Check health_metric_status before choosing a metric to build a page on, not after.
  • A status view reports the last day, so it can never show a hole in the middle. Apple Health went silent from Aug 20, 2026 at 11:17pm Eastern and did not post again until Aug 23 at 1:16am. Through the outage health_metric_status kept calling every live metric healthy, because it asks how quiet each metric is and its threshold was wider than the gap, so only health_feed_status showed it, all six feeds reporting the same last day. Now the feed is back, both views read healthy again, and the damage is invisible: Aug 21 has zero readings, and Aug 22 through Aug 25 hold 12 each against a normal day of about 25. Nothing in the database will ever raise this. A gap has to be caught while it is open, or counted day by day afterwards.
  • A locked table looks like an empty one. The four calendar_ tables, calendar_events, calendar_categories, calendar_inbox and calendar_sync_log, are the first in the system with anonymous read revoked, because pulling in three personal Google calendars means the table now holds private appointments. PostgREST answers a blocked read with an empty array and HTTP 200, not an error, so a signed-out browser renders a calendar with no events on it rather than a sign-in prompt. If the 2026 Calendar ever looks blank, check that you are signed in before you check anything else. worksheet_quotes is in the same state and is not yet wired to a page.
  • A column can exist, be documented, and still be empty on most rows. The social subsystem shipped with a full vocabulary for circles, labels, age bands and social groups, and connections now holds 155 events. circle is null on 115 of them. Social Orbit, Social Rings and Social Night Sky all draw by circle, so they render confidently off roughly a quarter of the history and nothing on the page says which quarter. Count the nulls in a classifying column before trusting anything grouped by it.
  • A score can be complete on every row and still be two-fifths of itself. ic_daily holds 1,039 days back to Mar 2019, and every one of them carries a score. Every one of them also has cognition, mood and senses set to null, because those three come from ic_checkin and nothing on the site writes to that table. domains_scored reads 2 on all 1,039 rows and says so plainly, but only if someone looks at it. A number that is never null is not the same as a number that is whole. Read the count-of-inputs column before reading the score.
  • Done can be a lie the table tells about itself. 48 tripit_trips rows are marked segments_status = done, stamped with a segments_pulled_at time and carrying a saved raw_page, and for five days tripit_segments held zero rows. A second pass over pages already in the database filled it, and it now holds 512 segments across 213 trips. The fetch succeeded and the parse wrote nothing, and the status column records the fetch, not the write. Whenever a status column and a destination table disagree, believe the destination table. Counting the rows on the far side is the only check that cannot be fooled.
  • An ok status can outlive the feed it describes. On Aug 28, 2026 health_metric_status reports eleven metrics as ok with the words Healthy. beside each one, and nothing has been written to health_metrics since Aug 26. The view is not lying: it computes days_quiet from the last day it holds, that came to 2, and its ok threshold is wider than 2. So the number the view shows is correct and the word beside it is wrong. This is the third form of the same fault: a status view cannot see a hole in the middle, it cannot see a shortfall, and its ok band is loose enough to cover a stopped feed. Read max(created_at), not the status word. The feed resumed on its own at 11:31pm Eastern on Aug 28, and the view now prints the same eleven Healthy. lines truthfully. The word did not change when the feed died and it did not change when the feed came back, which is the whole problem with it.
  • An ingest log can count one half of a payload. health_ingest_log rows 37 through 42 all record rows_received 0 and rows_written 0, which reads as six wasted posts. The Aug 27 post at 10:05am Eastern wrote two rows into health_workouts in that same second. The log counts readings only, so a post that carries workouts and no readings is filed as an empty one. A log that predates a feature will keep reporting the world as it was when it was written, so check what a log actually counts before reading a zero as nothing happened.
  • The first writer to a slot keeps it, and a ticked box beats a measurement. Four writers share qs_log, and sync_health_to_qs_log() skips any day and tracker that already has a row, whoever wrote it. The 4:00am habit reset writes a Walk row worth the value 1 and the word done, and it runs before the health data for that day has usually arrived. So when Apple Health caught up on Aug 28, 2026 with two days of totals, 7,821 steps, 14,678 steps and 53 exercise minutes were all dropped as duplicates and one row landed out of four. It repeated on Aug 29 with both of that day's totals. Since Jun 21, 2026, 112 day and tracker pairs qualified to be written, 27 never landed, and all 27 were blocked by a row another writer had already put there. Re-walking a window does not make a late arrival land if something else got to the slot first.
  • A receipt column can go bad quietly when no page reads it. calendar_pull_log records an unreadable count per paste, and this page said it was 0 on every pull, which meant the parser had never met a line it could not handle. Across 46 pulls that is no longer true: three left lines behind, 36 in all. Each of those pulls still reported success. Nothing on the site reads this table, so the only way the number was ever going to be seen was somebody opening it on purpose. A quality column is only a control if something actually looks at it.
  • Hidden is not deleted, and a guard only guards what comes next. A calendar Scott asked to keep out twice was blocked at the door on Aug 29, 2026 by a new list the parser reads before it saves. The 22 rows that had already arrived were flagged hidden instead, so the pages stopped showing them and the table still holds every one. A new filter never reaches backwards: check what is already stored, then decide whether hiding it is enough.
  • A headline is a claim about the present, and it can go stale between the check and the publish. On Sep 18, 2026 this page read the database at 6:12am, found Apple Health silent for thirty-one hours, and published stopped in its largest type at 7:21am. The feed had resumed at 6:16am, inside that gap. The reading was correct, the page was wrong, and no amount of verifying the upload would have caught it, because the upload was perfect. Re-read the one fact a headline rests on immediately before the final copy, not only at the start of the run.
  • A destination table with two writers cannot tell you that one of them has died. The Google calendar sync stopped after 3:09am on Sep 18, 2026 and calendar_events still grew, because the phone paste lane writes into the same table and had a busy day. The stop was visible only in calendar_sync_log, the sync's own receipt, sitting frozen at 622 runs. Judge a feed by its own receipt, never by the shared table it lands in, and be most suspicious when a shared table looks healthy.

DraftsThe mouth. You dictate, it posts the raw words to a Supabase function or waits for the 2-hourly sweep. It never sees GitHub and never writes a finished to-do, only a staged one.
