v2.6

How every outside source, Supabase and GitHub actually connect. Nine outside sources now write data, not two. Every table, view, function, trigger and scheduled task on this page was checked against the live database on Aug 24, 2026 at 6:15am Eastern. Two things moved overnight, and neither is a new source: a 2026 import review desk appeared in import_2026 with 47 rows staged and none promoted, and Apple Health is posting but only at half a normal day's volume, three days running. Click any box for what it holds and what breaks it.

The one-line version. Supabase is the whole system. Everything else is either a way of getting data into it (Drafts, Strava, the browser) or a way of getting code out of it (GitHub).

Data in. Ten sources write, and none of them talk to each other or touch GitHub. Each lands rows in Postgres, and the web pages simply read those tables. Drafts goes through a review gate first. Strava files itself straight in as already-completed. Apple Health is pushed off the phone by Health Auto Export, carrying the Apple Watch readings with it, because Apple offers no cloud API to pull from. The app is set to hourly, but the 32 posts logged so far landed anywhere from two hours to two days apart, so read it as a few times a day at best. It went quiet for two days, came back, and still has not recovered: nothing arrived between Aug 20, 2026 at 11:17pm and Aug 23, 2026 at 1:16am Eastern, Aug 21 has no readings at all, and Aug 22 through Aug 25 hold 11 or 12 each against roughly 25 on a normal day. Swarm pulls check-ins four times a day, promotes the countries it proves onto the travel map, and a database trigger turns every coffee stop into a qs_log row, which is where most of that table now comes from. The James Clear 3-2-1 archive is the odd one out: it was loaded once and nothing refreshes it. Farnam Street arrives on a weekly rhythm rather than a daily one. Google Calendar is the only source the system can write back to, so that arrow runs both directions. The iPhone calendar is the newest and the strangest: there is no connector and no schedule, the day is copied off the phone and pasted into a chat, and the paste is parsed into events. It exists because the Google sync only sees what Google knows about.

The map of itself. The knowledge graph tables are the odd one in: nothing outside writes them. They were filled by a single scan of the repo on Aug 16, 2026, looking for every .from('table') call a page makes, so they describe the site as it stood that day and drift a little every time a page changes.

New on Aug 19, 2026. Environmental Health (environmental.html) became the first page in the system that reads health_metrics, so the richest feed stopped being a dead end. It also brought the safaris table, the running list of places worth working from. Open Mode (open-mode.html) reads the written side of the knowledge graph plus cue_favorites.

New on Aug 21, 2026, and this is the bigger change. A brand new outside source is writing: Farnam Street's Brain Food, Shane Parrish's weekly newsletter. A scheduled task ingests each Monday issue into news_queue, The Feed (feed.html) shows it in his own section headers rather than ours, and starring an item copies it into farnam_street, the keeper shelf. Separately, health_metrics now flows onward: a third pg_cron job copies daily health totals into qs_log every two hours, so that table has four writers, not three. Three more pages arrived too, My Bucket List, Bucket List and Recreational Health, bringing bucket_list and the shared cue-card memory in deck_state and deck_progress.

New on Aug 22, 2026, and it was a subtraction. Apple Health stopped. The last Health Auto Export post landed Aug 20, 2026 at 11:17pm Eastern, which leaves health_metrics frozen at 16,954 rows and every one of its metrics reading a last day of Aug 20. Nothing errored and nothing looks broken, and health_metric_status still calls all thirteen live metrics healthy, so the only place the outage shows is health_feed_status, where all six feeds report the same last day. Downstream everything simply went quiet with it: qs_log has taken no new rows since Aug 21, because the two-hourly sync-health-to-qs job re-walks three days and finds nothing new to write. Elsewhere the week ticked over normally, and one small thing worked for the first time: farnam_street holds its first row, so the starring path off The Feed is now proven rather than assumed.

New on Aug 23, 2026, and it is the largest addition since this map was built. A ninth outside source is writing, and for the first time the traffic runs both ways. Google Calendar is synced every two hours across three calendars, Scottyex Default, Scott & Åsa and Family, into calendar_events, and anything created on the 2026 Calendar page (calendar.html, v1.3) is pushed back out to a named Google calendar. Every run writes a line to calendar_sync_log, in plain English rather than counters. Two one-off imports sit in the same table, the USC academic calendar (29 events) and the Greenwich Public Schools district calendar (39 events), and voice notes, URLs and PDFs land in calendar_inbox before they become events. This page is reached from Mission Control, not the top nav, and it is the first page in the system that will not load signed out, because the Google feed brings private appointments in with it. Separately, Habit Worksheets (habit-worksheets.html, v1.2) arrived as the new source of truth for which habits are live: its 74 catalogue rows and 36 section notes sit in worksheet_rows and worksheet_sections, and switching a row on writes it into daily_template, which is where the Clarity Compass and the habit board already read from. Two smaller pages brought tables of their own, the Nightly Scorecard (the dq_ tables) and the Recall Game (recall_attempts). And Apple Health came back at 1:16am Eastern, which closes the outage this page opened yesterday but leaves a hole in the middle of the week that no status view will ever mention.

New on Aug 26, 2026, and it is the biggest single addition this map has had to absorb. Twenty new tables and views arrived in two days, and most of them are one thing: the social subsystem. On Aug 24 connections held 2 rows and was a footnote inside the module tables box. It now holds 135 events across 45 people, with its own vocabulary (people_vocab, people_vocab_rules, people_known), its own birthdays table, three views that do the grouping and the name matching, and five pages reading them: Social (connections.html, v2.8), Social Orbit, Social Rings, Social Night Sky and Life Snapshot. Most of that history did not come through the import desk; 102 of the 135 events arrived in a keynote-2025 backfill. The one number to keep an eye on is circle, which is still null on 97 of the 135 rows, so the pages that draw by circle are drawing a quarter of the history confidently.

Two smaller subsystems landed with it. The day planner (day_blocks, day_tally, calendar_work_names) is the calendar column on Todays Tasks, and it is fed by a tenth way in: pasted phone calendar text, parsed in a session, logged to calendar_pull_log and stamped with pulled_at so a pulled event can be told from a stored one. Nothing on the site reads that log, which makes it the only ingest route on this map with no surface at all. And the cue-card streak (v_cue_card_day, v_cue_streak) now reports 9 days running, counted out of qs_log rather than out of the deck tables, against a hardcoded list of ten deck names that a new deck will not be on. Three older claims on this page have flipped and are corrected: habit_schedule is no longer empty, jc_321 is no longer frozen, and the 2026 import review page is no longer a 404.

Code out. The pages themselves live as gzipped blobs in a Supabase table. Writing one fires a database trigger that pokes GitHub, an Action decompresses it into the repo, and GitHub Pages is what your browser actually renders. Supabase stores the site, GitHub serves it.

Eleven things that have actually bitten

  • Grants are not policies. service_role once had no write access to any table, so the capture function returned HTTP 200 while storing nothing. Always write one real row as the real role.
  • Supabase will not render HTML. Anything on *.supabase.co is served as text/plain on purpose. That is why GitHub Pages, not the site function, is the live host. A custom domain is the only fix.
  • The local deploy folder goes stale. GitHub is canonical. Editing a local copy and publishing it has silently wiped live sections before.
  • The wheel does not read qs_log. Strava wrote to qs_log for days and nothing appeared, because nothing bridged it to todos. The wheel reads todos plus time_log, and nothing else.
  • Scheduled sessions do reach Supabase, and this page said otherwise for two days. A scheduled cloud session audited and republished this page on Aug 18, 2026 through the ordinary Supabase connector, and the nightly map check writes its findings into session_todos at 3:24am. What a scheduled run cannot reach is the Mac, so it has no Cowork project memory and no local tools. The Midnight Run is still silent, but this was never the reason.
  • A shared table can be empty and still look like it works. deck_progress is how every cue-card page remembers what it already showed you, and for two days it held zero rows. A page with no memory treats every tile as unseen, so the cards render perfectly while the rotation does nothing at all. Nothing errors and nothing looks wrong. The only way to see it was to count the rows. It cleared on Aug 22, 2026 and now holds 37 rows across five decks, with 2 rows in deck_state, which means two decks have been read all the way to the end. The whole thing works now; it is kept here because the failure mode was invisible, not because it is still live.
  • A row count going up is not proof the page is being used. safaris went from 1 row to 18 in two days, which reads like adoption. In fact 17 of those carry source = manual and were loaded straight through the database; only one came from anyone typing into the page. Check source before you read growth as usage.
  • A metric can exist and still be empty. health_metrics is watched for 16 metrics and only 13 have ever arrived: weight, body fat and lean body mass have never appeared at all, and two more are still flagged thin, mindful minutes at 2 readings and resting heart rate at 13. Environmental Health was built on time_in_daylight, which has crept up to 14 and now reads as healthy, which shows how low that threshold sits. Check health_metric_status before choosing a metric to build a page on, not after.
  • A status view reports the last day, so it can never show a hole in the middle. Apple Health went silent from Aug 20, 2026 at 11:17pm Eastern and did not post again until Aug 23 at 1:16am. Through the outage health_metric_status kept calling every live metric healthy, because it asks how quiet each metric is and its threshold was wider than the gap, so only health_feed_status showed it, all six feeds reporting the same last day. Now the feed is back, both views read healthy again, and the damage is invisible: Aug 21 has zero readings, and Aug 22 through Aug 25 hold 11 or 12 each against a normal day of about 25. Five days on, every metric still reports days_quiet 1 and status ok. Nothing in the database will ever raise this. A gap has to be caught while it is open, or counted day by day afterwards.
  • A locked table looks like an empty one. The four calendar_ tables are the first in the system with anonymous read revoked, because pulling in three personal Google calendars means the table now holds private appointments. PostgREST answers a blocked read with an empty array and HTTP 200, not an error, so a signed-out browser renders a calendar with no events on it rather than a sign-in prompt. If the 2026 Calendar ever looks blank, check that you are signed in before you check anything else. worksheet_quotes is in the same state and is not yet wired to a page.
  • A column can exist, be documented, and still be empty on most rows. The social subsystem shipped with a full vocabulary for circles, labels, age bands and social groups, and connections now holds 135 events. circle is null on 97 of them. Social Orbit, Social Rings and Social Night Sky all draw by circle, so they render confidently off roughly a quarter of the history and nothing on the page says which quarter. Count the nulls in a classifying column before trusting anything grouped by it.

The seven named pieces, in one sentence each

PieceIts actual job
DraftsThe mouth. You dictate, it posts the raw words to a Supabase function or waits for the 2-hourly sweep. It never sees GitHub and never writes a finished to-do, only a staged one.
StravaA sensor. A scheduled task pulls your activities and writes them into todos as already-completed rows so the Time Bandit Wheel can draw them. Read-only from Strava's side.
SupabaseThe whole system: the database every page reads, the auth, the functions that receive captures, and the store the site's own HTML lives in.
Farnam StreetA reading habit, wired up like a feed. A weekly task fetches Shane Parrish's Brain Food into news_queue, The Feed shows it in his sections rather than ours, and starring copies it onto the farnam_street shelf. It is pull, not push, so it has no keys and no webhook.
Google CalendarThe only two-way door in the system. A task pulls three of his calendars in every two hours and pushes anything created here back out, so the same event can be edited on a phone or on the page and end up in both. It is also the reason one page now requires a sign-in.
iPhone calendarThe manual door. There is no connector and no schedule: the day is copied off the phone, pasted into a chat, and parsed into events stamped with pulled_at. It exists because the Google sync only sees the calendars Google knows about, and it runs only when you run it.
GitHubThe printing press. It holds no data of its own. A database trigger pokes it, an Action unzips the pages out of Supabase into the repo, and GitHub Pages serves them to your browser.
