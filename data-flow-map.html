How every outside source, Supabase and GitHub actually connect. Fourteen outside sources now write data, not two. Every table, view, function, trigger and scheduled task on this page was checked against the live database on Sep 4, 2026 at 6:15am Eastern. This was the largest single night this map has ever had to absorb: nineteen new tables and three new views. The new way in is Photo Booth, which reads the pictures off the phone and the Mac and files a chosen shot as a tracked daily habit. Two good pieces of news: Apple Health started writing readings again on Sep 3, 2026 at 11:21pm Eastern after two quiet days, and the needs-you.js pop-up that no page loaded yesterday is now loaded on every page on the site, which is why all four open questions have been answered. The schema is 125 tables and 40 views, pg_cron runs thirteen jobs and public holds twenty-seven triggers. Click any box for what it holds and what breaks it.

The one-line version. Supabase is the whole system. Everything else is either a way of getting data into it (Drafts, Strava, the browser) or a way of getting code out of it (GitHub).

Data in. Fourteen sources write, and none of them talk to each other or touch GitHub. Each lands rows in Postgres, and the web pages simply read those tables. Drafts goes through a review gate first. Strava files itself straight in as already-completed. Apple Health is pushed off the phone by Health Auto Export, carrying the Apple Watch readings with it, because Apple offers no cloud API to pull from. The app is set to hourly, but the 89 posts logged so far landed anywhere from two hours to two days apart, so read it as a few times a day at best. The readings came back on their own on Sep 3, 2026 at 11:21pm Eastern, 28 received and 26 written, after two days in which every post carried walks and no readings at all. That is the third time this feed has been called quiet and come back without anyone touching it. It has now gone quiet twice and come back twice: nothing arrived between Aug 20, 2026 at 11:17pm and Aug 23, 2026 at 1:16am Eastern, then nothing again from Aug 26 at 8:24pm until Aug 28 at 11:31pm. Aug 21 has no readings at all and Aug 22 through Aug 26 hold 12 or 13 each against roughly 25 on a normal day, so the recovery does not repair the middle of last week. Swarm pulls check-ins four times a day, promotes the countries it proves onto the travel map, and a database trigger turns every coffee stop into a qs_log row, which is where most of that table now comes from. Its newest check-in is Sep 3, 2026 at 6:13pm Eastern, and unlike Strava it keeps no log of its own, so the database cannot say whether the lane stopped or the days were quiet. The James Clear 3-2-1 archive is the odd one out: it was loaded once and nothing refreshes it. Farnam Street arrives on a weekly rhythm rather than a daily one. Google Calendar is the only source the system can write back to, so that arrow runs both directions. Gondola and TripIt are read out of a browser by hand and never refresh themselves. The iPhone calendar is the strangest of all: there is no connector and no schedule, the day is copied off the phone and pasted into a chat, and the paste is parsed into events. It exists because the Google sync only sees what Google knows about.

New on Aug 31, 2026, and it is the first thing on this map that has no clock and no session behind it. Three database triggers went in this afternoon, and a trigger is a kind of machinery this page had never drawn. Nothing on the site calls one and nothing schedules one: it runs in the same instant as the row that set it off, and if it fails the row fails with it. The first, trg_health_workout_to_todo, turns an Apple Health workout into a ticked to-do the moment it lands, so a walk recorded on the watch now reaches the board instead of stopping inside health_workouts. The second, trg_strava_todo_to_day_block, puts any activity to-do onto the calendar column of Todays Tasks as a block in day_blocks. That second one is bigger than it sounds. Until today day_blocks had exactly one writer, the browser, and only when Scott physically ticked a row on the page, so nothing running in the cloud could put anything on his calendar at all. It now has two writers and the new one never opens the site. The third, trg_activity_row_dedupe, exists because Apple Health and Strava both report the same walk hours apart: whichever arrives second upgrades the row already there instead of adding a twin, matched on a start time within five minutes and the same sport emoji. A helper, recompute_cardio_qs(date), re-adds the day's Cardio miles afterwards, so the total comes out right whichever feed filled the row.

And the way those three arrived is the newest trap on this page. The daily audit behind this map counts what is in the database by walking its catalog of tables and views. A trigger is not a table and a helper function is not a view, so neither one is ever counted. An entire working subsystem can ship, change what the calendar shows and what the board holds, and the schema count on this page will read exactly the same as it did the day before. There are 27 triggers in public today: three came with the Food Log on the night of Sep 1, 2026, three more with the Asks queue the night after, and six more overnight on Sep 3, including photo_shot_to_qs, which is what turns a filed photograph into a tracked habit. When the site starts behaving differently and no table has changed, a trigger is the first place to look, and the only way to find one is to ask the database for its triggers by name.

New on Sep 2, 2026, and it is the first thing here that asks you a question. Asks is one queue for the whole system: a module that gets stuck on something only you can settle writes a row into asks with a short question in plain English and up to four tappable answers, and the answer routes back to the row it came from through a database trigger. Nothing in the queue knows anything about food, but everything in it so far came from the Food Log, because reading a plate from a photograph is the first job on this system that regularly runs out of certainty. Two of the four have been answered, both on Sep 2, 2026 at 9:09am Eastern.

And the trap is the other half, closed the day after it was named. The pop-up written to show those questions, needs-you.js, was finished, published to the pages table and sitting in the repo, and on Sep 3, 2026 not one page on the site loaded it. Not a single HTML file referenced it, navpatch.js did not, and the publish Action that injects the navigation into every page did not either. It is fixed: navpatch.js now injects needs-you.js itself, so the pop-up reaches every page the navigation reaches, and all four questions in the queue have since been answered. The fix was one line in the file that was already going everywhere. It also fails silent by design, which is right for a pop-up and wrong for a diagnosis: a script that is never loaded looks exactly like a script with nothing to say. So this is a third way for work to be finished and invisible, and the quietest of the three. A page missing from the navigation can still be reached by URL. A page with no version badge is at least in the navigation. A shared script that nothing loads has no symptom at all. The Food Log page happens to show its own meal questions from meals, which is how two got answered; the one question that points at no row has nowhere to appear.

Code out. The pages themselves live as gzipped blobs in a Supabase table. Writing one fires a database trigger that pokes GitHub, an Action decompresses it into the repo, and GitHub Pages is what your browser actually renders. Supabase stores the site, GitHub serves it.

Twenty-three things that have actually bitten

  • The same fact in two tables is worse than the fact in none. Water is written by the Food Log and Log a Meal into water_log, and by Habit Modules into water_glasses. Both shipped on the night of Sep 3, 2026, nothing joins them, and neither page reads the other's table. Four glasses were logged that evening through Habit Modules, so water_glasses holds four rows, water_log holds none, and the Food Log reports no water for a day the water was actually logged. v_food_day rolls the day up out of water_log alone and so repeats the zero, and no page reads that view, so nothing on the site ever shows the two halves side by side. Coffee is one step behind the same fault: 595 rows of history sit in qs_log and a new empty coffee_cups sits beside them. An empty table is easy to spot; a table that is right about the wrong half is not. Before adding a store for something, search the schema for what already stores it.
  • A shared script that nothing loads has no symptom at all, and it was fixed the next day. needs-you.js was written on Sep 2, 2026 to show the open questions in asks on whatever page you happen to be on. It was finished, in the pages table and in the repo, and on Sep 3 no HTML file on the site referenced it, nor did navpatch.js, nor did the publish Action that injects the navigation everywhere. It is also built to fail silent, so there was nothing to see and nothing to log. It is loaded now: navpatch.js injects it, so it reaches every page the navigation reaches, and all four questions in the queue have since been answered. The lesson outlives the fix, because the only thing that ever found it was a text search of the repo for the file name. The three ways work can be finished and invisible run in order of quietness: a page missing from the navigation can still be reached by URL, a page with no version badge is at least in the navigation, and a shared script nothing loads produces no evidence of any kind. Publishing a script is two steps, and the second one is loading it.
  • A view that returns nothing is usually telling the truth. v_social_fitness shipped on Aug 30, 2026 and returns zero rows. Nothing is wrong with the view: it reads qs_log for a tracker named Social Fitness, and no row in qs_log carries that tracker, because nothing has been logged against the deck yet. The instinct on an empty result is to suspect the join. Check whether the value exists in the source table first, because most of the time the answer is that the thing being measured has not happened.
  • A blocked feed and a quiet feed look identical from the destination table. On Aug 31, 2026 three of the four Reddit sources behind The Wall answered http 403, at 3:24, 3:28 and 3:32am Eastern. The jobs did not error, nothing on the site changed, and wall_items simply did not grow, which is exactly what it does on a morning with nothing new. The only record anywhere is wall_sources.last_pull_note, a text column no page reads. If a feed can be refused, something has to store the refusal, or the outage is unobservable.
  • A page can be live and completely unreachable. wall.html, wall-saved.html and social-fitness.html all shipped on Aug 30, 2026, all sit in the pages table with a payload, and all render. None of the three is listed in nav-config.js, so none appears in the navigation anywhere on the site, and none carries a version badge, so the version sweep cannot see them either. three-new-things.html and now photo-booth.html are in the same state, which makes five, and this has stayed true for six days running. Publishing a page is three steps, not one, and the row count going from 110 to 115 proves only the first.
  • Grants are not policies. service_role once had no write access to any table, so the capture function returned HTTP 200 while storing nothing. Always write one real row as the real role.
  • Supabase will not render HTML. Anything on *.supabase.co is served as text/plain on purpose. That is why GitHub Pages, not the site function, is the live host. A custom domain is the only fix.
  • The local deploy folder goes stale. GitHub is canonical. Editing a local copy and publishing it has silently wiped live sections before.
  • The wheel does not read qs_log. Strava wrote to qs_log for days and nothing appeared, because nothing bridged it to todos. The wheel reads todos plus time_log, and nothing else.
  • Scheduled sessions do reach Supabase, and this page said otherwise for two days. A scheduled cloud session audited and republished this page on Aug 18, 2026 through the ordinary Supabase connector, and the nightly map check writes its findings into session_todos at 3:24am. What a scheduled run cannot reach is the Mac, so it has no Cowork project memory and no local tools. The Midnight Run is still silent, but this was never the reason.
  • A shared table can be empty and still look like it works. deck_progress is how every cue-card page remembers what it already showed you, and for two days it held zero rows. A page with no memory treats every tile as unseen, so the cards render perfectly while the rotation does nothing at all. Nothing errors and nothing looks wrong. The only way to see it was to count the rows. It cleared on Aug 22, 2026 and now holds 87 rows across five decks, with 3 rows in deck_state, which means three decks have been read all the way to the end. The whole thing works now; it is kept here because the failure mode was invisible, not because it is still live.
  • A row count going up is not proof the page is being used. safaris went from 1 row to 18 in two days, which reads like adoption. In fact 17 of those carry source = manual and were loaded straight through the database; only one came from anyone typing into the page. Check source before you read growth as usage.
  • A metric can exist and still be empty. health_metrics is watched for 16 metrics and only 13 have ever arrived: weight, body fat and lean body mass have never appeared at all, and two more are still flagged thin, mindful minutes at 2 readings and resting heart rate at 18. Environmental Health was built on time_in_daylight, which has crept up to 19 and now reads as healthy, which shows how low that threshold sits. Check health_metric_status before choosing a metric to build a page on, not after.
  • A status view reports the last day, so it can never show a hole in the middle. Apple Health went silent from Aug 20, 2026 at 11:17pm Eastern and did not post again until Aug 23 at 1:16am. Through the outage health_metric_status kept calling every live metric healthy, because it asks how quiet each metric is and its threshold was wider than the gap, so only health_feed_status showed it, all six feeds reporting the same last day. Now the feed is back, both views read healthy again, and the damage is invisible: Aug 21 has zero readings, and Aug 22 through Aug 25 hold 12 each against a normal day of about 25. Nothing in the database will ever raise this. A gap has to be caught while it is open, or counted day by day afterwards.
  • A locked table looks like an empty one. The four calendar_ tables, calendar_events, calendar_categories, calendar_inbox and calendar_sync_log, are the first in the system with anonymous read revoked, because pulling in three personal Google calendars means the table now holds private appointments. PostgREST answers a blocked read with an empty array and HTTP 200, not an error, so a signed-out browser renders a calendar with no events on it rather than a sign-in prompt. If the 2026 Calendar ever looks blank, check that you are signed in before you check anything else. worksheet_quotes is in the same state and is not yet wired to a page.
  • A column can exist, be documented, and still be empty on most rows. The social subsystem shipped with a full vocabulary for circles, labels, age bands and social groups, and connections now holds 154 events. circle is null on 115 of them. Social Orbit, Social Rings and Social Night Sky all draw by circle, so they render confidently off roughly a quarter of the history and nothing on the page says which quarter. Count the nulls in a classifying column before trusting anything grouped by it.
  • A score can be complete on every row and still be two-fifths of itself. ic_daily holds 1,021 days back to Mar 2019, and every one of them carries a score. Every one of them also has cognition, mood and senses set to null, because those three come from ic_checkin and nothing on the site writes to that table. domains_scored reads 2 on all 1,021 rows and says so plainly, but only if someone looks at it. A number that is never null is not the same as a number that is whole. Read the count-of-inputs column before reading the score.
  • Done can be a lie the table tells about itself. 48 tripit_trips rows are marked segments_status = done, stamped with a segments_pulled_at time and carrying a saved raw_page, and tripit_segments holds zero rows. The fetch succeeded and the parse wrote nothing, and the status column records the fetch, not the write. Whenever a status column and a destination table disagree, believe the destination table. Counting the rows on the far side is the only check that cannot be fooled.
  • An ok status can outlive the feed it describes. On Aug 28, 2026 health_metric_status reports eleven metrics as ok with the words Healthy. beside each one, and nothing has been written to health_metrics since Aug 26. The view is not lying: it computes days_quiet from the last day it holds, that came to 2, and its ok threshold is wider than 2. So the number the view shows is correct and the word beside it is wrong. This is the third form of the same fault: a status view cannot see a hole in the middle, it cannot see a shortfall, and its ok band is loose enough to cover a stopped feed. Read max(created_at), not the status word. The feed resumed on its own at 11:31pm Eastern on Aug 28, and the view now prints the same eleven Healthy. lines truthfully. The word did not change when the feed died and it did not change when the feed came back, which is the whole problem with it.
  • An ingest log can count one half of a payload. health_ingest_log rows 37 through 42 all record rows_received 0 and rows_written 0, which reads as six wasted posts. The Aug 27 post at 10:05am Eastern wrote two rows into health_workouts in that same second. The log counts readings only, so a post that carries workouts and no readings is filed as an empty one. A log that predates a feature will keep reporting the world as it was when it was written, so check what a log actually counts before reading a zero as nothing happened.
  • The first writer to a slot keeps it, and a ticked box beats a measurement. Four writers share qs_log, and sync_health_to_qs_log() skips any day and tracker that already has a row, whoever wrote it. The 4:00am habit reset writes a Walk row worth the value 1 and the word done, and it runs before the health data for that day has usually arrived. So when Apple Health caught up on Aug 28, 2026 with two days of totals, 7,821 steps, 14,678 steps and 53 exercise minutes were all dropped as duplicates and one row landed out of four. It repeated on Aug 29 with both of that day's totals. Since Jun 21, 2026, 112 day and tracker pairs qualified to be written, 27 never landed, and all 27 were blocked by a row another writer had already put there. Re-walking a window does not make a late arrival land if something else got to the slot first.
  • A receipt column can go bad quietly when no page reads it. calendar_pull_log records an unreadable count per paste, and this page said it was 0 on every pull, which meant the parser had never met a line it could not handle. Across 46 pulls that is no longer true: three left lines behind, 36 in all. Each of those pulls still reported success. Nothing on the site reads this table, so the only way the number was ever going to be seen was somebody opening it on purpose. A quality column is only a control if something actually looks at it.
  • Hidden is not deleted, and a guard only guards what comes next. A calendar Scott asked to keep out twice was blocked at the door on Aug 29, 2026 by a new list the parser reads before it saves. The 22 rows that had already arrived were flagged hidden instead, so the pages stopped showing them and the table still holds every one. A new filter never reaches backwards: check what is already stored, then decide whether hiding it is enough.

The eight named pieces, in one sentence each

PieceIts actual job
DraftsThe mouth. You dictate, it posts the raw words to a Supabase function or waits for the 2-hourly sweep. It never sees GitHub and never writes a finished to-do, only a staged one.
StravaA sensor. A scheduled task pulls your activities and writes them into todos as already-completed rows so the Time Bandit Wheel can draw them. Read-only from Strava's side.
SupabaseThe whole system: the database every page reads, the auth, the functions that receive captures, and the store the site's own HTML lives in.
Farnam StreetA reading habit, wired up like a feed. A weekly task fetches Shane Parrish's Brain Food into news_queue, The Feed shows it in his sections rather than ours, and starring copies it onto the farnam_street shelf. It is pull, not push, so it has no keys and no webhook.
Google CalendarThe only two-way door in the system. A task pulls three of his calendars in every two hours and pushes anything created here back out, so the same event can be edited on a phone or on the page and end up in both. It is also the reason one page now requires a sign-in.
iPhone calendarThe manual door. There is no connector and no schedule: the day is copied off the phone, pasted into a chat, and parsed into events stamped with pulled_at. It exists because the Google sync only sees the calendars Google knows about, and it runs only when you run it.
TripItThe archive. Twenty years of trips, read out of a browser once and parked in its own two tables, deliberately not merged into the travel map until it has been reconciled against Swarm. It has no key, no webhook and no schedule, so it is only as current as the last time somebody read it.
GitHubThe printing press. It holds no data of its own. A database trigger pokes it, an Action unzips the pages out of Supabase into the repo, and GitHub Pages serves them to your browser.
