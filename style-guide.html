Letter spacing. Only capitals get extra spacing. Small caps get .5px, a kicker gets 1.6px, a card section label gets .07em. Ordinary sentences get none.

Space

How wide a page runs and how far things sit apart. Pick the width by what the page is for, not by what looks nice on the day.
Page widths
560px: one thing at a timeA single card, a form, a question. Reading stays easy because the line never gets long.
760px: readingDocuments, guardrails, anything that is mostly words.
1000px: the standard pageMission Control, this page, most modules. The default unless there is a reason.
1480px, or 94% of the screen: boardsOnly the Cue Cards board and the big maps, where more cards across is the point.
Gaps
Grids. Cards in a grid are set to a smallest width and told to fill: 210px for module tiles, 268px for cue cards, 168px for colour swatches. That is what makes a board go two-up on a laptop and five across on a big screen without a single media query.

Corners

Bigger the thing, rounder the corner. Anything that holds a word or two is a full lozenge; anything round is a dot.

Shadows

Four levels, and that is the whole set. The higher a thing floats, the more important it is meant to feel. Everything is the same blue-grey, never black.

Buttons

One blue button per screen: the thing you actually came to do. Everything else is outlined. Buttons are 9px rounded, bold, and never wider than they need to be unless they sit in a sign-in box.

Every page has these, in this order

If a page is missing one of these it is not finished. Nothing here is hand-built any more. The top bar is injected, and a page with no nav markup at all still gets the full bar.
1 · Sign-in box340px wide, floats in the middle, level 4 shadow. It says which page you are signing in to. Same on all 27 pages that have one.
2 · The top barOne nav for the whole site, built from one file. You never edit it page by page. Adding, removing or renaming anything is one line in nav-config.js. The bar holds sections; a page that lives on a hub does not need a slot.
3 · Page headerTitle in blue at 24, version chip, today's date. Always in that order, always at the top of the content.
4 · One line saying what the page is forGrey, 13.5px, never wider than 760px. Plain English, no jargon.
5 · The contentCards down the page, 18px apart.
6 · Version badge, bottom rightFixed to the corner. This is the one place the version number is really declared. Every other version on the page is a copy of it. Typing a number into a copy is a bug.
7 · ToastA dark bar that slides up from the bottom middle for two seconds to confirm something saved. Never used for errors that need reading.
8 · Empty stateWhen there is nothing to show, say so in words. Never leave a blank space.

Icons

One shared set, 69 of them, held in a single file every page can read. They are small pictures rather than a font, so they look the same everywhere. If you need a new one it gets added here, not drawn on a page.

The rules

Not preferences. These were each written down after something went wrong.
1Plain English, everywhere.Chat, page copy, button labels, card copy, and the message a button gives when it will not work. No research, no jargon, no term you would have to look up. Say the answer first.
2One thing, one name.The same name on the page, in the nav, in the database, in the docs. When two names exist, one of them is wrong and gets fixed everywhere at once.
3The version goes up on every change.Declared in one place per page: the badge in the bottom-right corner. Everything else showing a version is a copy of it.
4Never refuse silently.A button that will not do the thing has to say what it is waiting for, in one line, before it is pressed. No dead grey rectangles.
5The top bar holds sections, not pages.A page that lives on a hub does not need a slot in the nav. The hub is its nav.
6One blue button per screen.The thing you came to do. Everything else is outlined or plain.
7A headline is never repeated in the body.The card is small. The body adds the example or the point. It never says the headline again.
8Dimmed means asleep, and asleep is small.A finished or parked card shrinks to a quarter height. It must never look as important as a live one.
9Never invent a colour.If the thing you are building needs a colour that is not on this page, the answer is to add it here first, not to type a new hex into a page.
