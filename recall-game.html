Where this came from

The idea. Scott asked for a game built from two coaching books. The better answer was a game built from all of them. His Knowledge Graph holds notes from fifteen sources mapped onto the seven parts of his Clarity Compass, and until now there was no way to check whether any of it had stuck. Reading is not remembering. So this plays over the whole corpus rather than one book, which also means every source he adds in future arrives with a game already attached, at no extra build cost.

Why it asks these two questions. Showing a practice and hiding where it came from is retrieval practice: pulling something out of memory strengthens it far more than reading it again does. Asking which life area it belongs to is the more useful of the two, because it tests whether he has internalised his own model rather than just the book.

What it records. Every answer is logged, so the breakdown at the end shows accuracy by life area. Over a few rounds that reveals which parts of his own Compass he cannot recall, which is a more honest signal than any reading list. Notes answered wrongly are weighted to come back sooner.

Sources. Everything on screen is live from the Knowledge Graph tables, written and approved over time. Two arrived today: Michael Bungay Stanier, The Coaching Habit (2016) as S13, and Marshall Goldsmith, Triggers: Creating Behavior That Lasts (2015) as S14. The retrieval practice and spacing effects behind the format are long standing findings in cognitive psychology, not a claim from either book.
