Where this came from

The idea. Scott asked how to get two coaching books into Project YOU as something interactive rather than something to read. The answer turned on a distinction: most of his corpus is ideas to absorb, where a card you read is the right container, but these two books are practices to run. A question you ask, a score you give. Putting a verb on a card you read is why most people finish these two books, nod, and change nothing. So instead of a sixth cue card, this is the actual nightly ritual from the book, wired into the tracking that was already here.

The method is Goldsmith's, not an interpretation of it. The wording ("Did I do my best to…"), the one to ten scale, and the idea of an outside voice reading the list aloud are all his. His published set is six questions; he personally answers 43 every day and pays someone to phone him and ask them.

What was designed here. Three questions anchor every night so the trend means something, and three rotate from a pool of fifteen so it does not go stale. Because the set changes, the headline number is a percentage rather than a raw total, otherwise a six question night and a four question night could not be compared. A missed night is forgiven once in any seven, because a streak that snaps on the first bad evening does not survive a bad week.
